# Limitations

- County-level averages can hide major differences between cities, suburbs, and rural areas within the same county.
- The model uses data from different underlying years: population and age are primarily 2023; diabetes and PCP measures primarily reflect 2022 and 2021 data, respectively.
- Primary-care physician and other-primary-care-provider rates measure registered supply, not appointment capacity, wait time, hours, network participation, or acceptance of new patients.
- The score uses analyst-selected weights and percentile ranks. It is transparent but not empirically validated.
- A population threshold of 50,000 is used only to avoid recommending very small counties from rate-based metrics.
- Median household income is a commercial-context proxy, not a measure of healthcare need.
- The analysis excludes payer mix, reimbursement, labor costs, real estate, patient travel patterns, competitor capacity, licensing, capital requirements, revenue, and profitability.
