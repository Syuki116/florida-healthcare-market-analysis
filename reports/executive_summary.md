# Executive Summary

## Decision

Prioritize **Sumter, St. Lucie, and Flagler counties** for the next round of outpatient-clinic market research under the Balanced scenario. All three remain in the Top 10 under the Balanced, Healthcare Need Focus, and Growth Focus scenarios, making them less dependent on one weighting choice.

## Why these counties surfaced

- **Sumter County** ranks first in the Balanced and Growth scenarios. It combines the fastest recent population growth among the leading counties, an unusually high 65+ population share, below-median primary-care supply, and above-median household income. Its diabetes prevalence is not especially high, so its case is driven more by growth and age-related outpatient demand.
- **St. Lucie County** ranks second in both the Balanced and Growth scenarios and second in the Healthcare Need scenario. It combines strong growth, above-median diabetes prevalence, an older-than-state-median population, and low PCP supply.
- **Flagler County** ranks third in the Balanced and Growth scenarios and seventh under Healthcare Need. It combines strong growth, a large 65+ population, and low PCP and other-primary-care-provider supply.

## Important alternatives

**Polk, Osceola, and Lake counties** also remain in the Top 10 across all three scenarios. Polk and Osceola offer much larger populations and high diabetes prevalence, while Lake combines growth, older residents, and relatively constrained provider supply. These counties may be stronger choices for organizations that value market scale over the highest composite score.

## Recommendation

Use the score as a first-pass shortlist, then conduct local due diligence in the six stable Top-10 counties: Sumter, St. Lucie, Flagler, Polk, Osceola, and Lake. The next analysis should add sub-county population growth, competitor locations and capacity, payer mix, staffing availability, real-estate costs, travel times, and expected reimbursement.

## Limitation

The analysis does not estimate patient volume, revenue, profitability, or optimal street-level location. Provider rates are supply proxies and do not measure appointment availability or acceptance of new patients.
